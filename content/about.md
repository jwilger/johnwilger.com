I’m John Wilger, Head of Engineering at [10kR](https://10kr.co/), where we build AI products and experiences. I set technical direction, work alongside engineers on hard problems, and mentor people learning to make those decisions themselves. You can see [what 10kR builds](https://10kr.co/#work) on the company’s site.

I’ve spent more than 25 years building software and leading engineering teams. Before 10kR, I was VP of Engineering and a Principal Engineering Consultant at [Artium](https://artium.ai/), where I worked with client teams on how they build software and mentored fellow consultants.

## How I work

I try to make the important rules of a system explicit. Event modeling lets a team work through what actually happens before we commit to an implementation. Event sourcing makes changes to state traceable. Tests give us evidence about how the system behaves; mutation testing can show when those tests would miss a change that matters.

I apply the same reasoning when I use AI. I’ll delegate implementation, including work I wouldn’t have taken on otherwise. I still have to decide what counts as correct, what the tools are allowed to do, and how I’ll know whether a change is ready to ship. [Somebody Has to Design the Factory](/blog/somebody-has-to-design-the-factory/) is a write-up of what that looks like in my own work.

For hands-on examples, see my [open-source projects](/projects/). For more about technical decisions and the teams that make them, see [Perspectives](/blog/).
